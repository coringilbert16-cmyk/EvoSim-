# PLAN-006 — Spatially organized chemistry

**Status:** Planned; controlled spatial comparison and adequate local diagnostics are prerequisites.

## Research question

Do flow, resonance propagation, energy gradients, and transport create persistent spatial reaction zones or connected chemical pathways that aggregate chemistry statistics would hide?

## Required evidence

1. Record spatially resolved molecule positions, concentrations, reaction events, and local field/energy exposure over time.
2. Define reproducible spatial conditions, such as baseline versus altered flow or field geometry, with matched inventories and seeds.
3. Compare local reaction rates and pathway/family diversity after normalizing for local reactant availability and contact opportunity.
4. Track the formation, movement, persistence, and disappearance of reaction zones.
5. Distinguish effects of environmental physics from placement artifacts or unequal material distributions.
6. Preserve full material and energy accounting.

## Candidate variables

- Flow direction, speed, and geometry.
- Resonance-source placement and field exposure.
- Spatial energy gradients and transport.
- Initial distribution of reactants, with matched total inventories.

These are experimental variables, not proposed chemistry-specific reaction rules.

## Infrastructure prerequisites

The Chemistry Laboratory exposes flow and source controls and supports deterministic tick-zero placement. However, it does not currently provide automated matched multi-condition execution. The existing environmental workflow is baseline-only. Add controlled comparison execution and spatially resolved opportunity/rate diagnostics before running or interpreting intervention experiments.

## Interpretation guardrails

A spatial hotspot in raw event counts may simply reflect more reactants or more contacts in that region. Claims of spatial organization require exposure-normalized local rates and persistence across independent seeds.
