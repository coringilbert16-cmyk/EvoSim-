## 2. Shared field and work contract

For the approved dimensionless scalar field,

\[
u_{tt}=c^2\nabla^2u+f(\mathbf{x},t),
\qquad
e=\frac K2(u_t^2+c^2|\nabla u|^2),
\qquad
\mathbf J=-Kc^2u_t\nabla u.
\]

The local field-energy balance is

\[
\partial_t e+\nabla\cdot\mathbf J=K u_t f.
\]

Therefore the signed power density delivered to the field by forcing is

\[
p_{\mathrm{field}}(\mathbf{x},t)=K u_t f
\quad [E/(L^2\,tick)].
\]

For a source profile \(g_s\) and scalar forcing amplitude \(a_s\), Candidate A uses \(f_s=a_s(t)g_s(\mathbf{x})\). The integrated signed source work is determined by the actual field velocity and forcing:

\[
P_s(t)=\int K u_t f_s\,dA.
\]

Positive work transfers energy from the source account to the field. Negative work transfers energy from the field back to the source. The exact leapfrog discrete identity and atomic settlement are validated in the isolated solver.

This contract is necessary for accounting, but it does not tell us how a physical source with a specified incident power should generate \(f_s\). Prescribing a positive power density \(q_s\) and solving \(K u_t f_s=q_s\) is singular at field-velocity nodes and can demand unbounded forcing. Clamping negative work or forcing only at selected phases would change the model and is not an acceptable shortcut.

## 3. Three source classes — separate physical laws

### 3.1 External radiative sun

**Approved geometry and clock**
- The sun is an external isotropic three-dimensional radiative source above the infinite two-dimensional plane.
- Its orbit is a vertical circle in a plane perpendicular to the world plane; one orbit is 24,000 ticks, equal to one simulated day.
- The carrier completes 440 cycles per day. Orbital phase and carrier phase are separate state variables.
- Source-to-plane propagation is finite-speed and uses retarded emission time.
- An infinite plane intercepts one hemisphere of isotropic emission: one half of total emitted energy. The other half remains in the external accounting domain.
- The source is not a line emitter, a boundary emitter, or a material oscillator.

For a stationary isotropic point source at height \(h\), distance \(d\), emitted power \(P_{\odot}\), and a planar point with incidence cosine \(h/d\), the instantaneous incident power density has the geometric form

\[
q_{\odot}(\mathbf{x},t)
=\frac{P_{\odot}(t_{emit})}{4\pi d^2}\frac{h}{d}
=\frac{P_{\odot}(t_{emit})h}{4\pi d^3},
\qquad [q_{\odot}]=E/(L^2\,tick).
\]

For a moving orbiting source, the implementation must also reconcile emission-time intervals with arrival-time intervals. The retarded-time map is \(t=\tau+d(\tau,\mathbf{x})/c\); its time Jacobian must be considered when converting emitted power per source tick into received power per plane tick. The half-power statement is an integrated energy/geometric constraint, not permission to ignore retarded-time bookkeeping. The exact convention for isotropic emission in the project frame and the corresponding moving-source flux factor must be made explicit and tested.

**Unresolved coupling question:** \(q_{\odot}\) is incident radiative power per physical plane area. It is not the wave-equation forcing \(f\), nor is it automatically equal to \(K u_t f\). The physical mechanism that converts intercepted external radiation into the planar scalar field has not yet been specified. Candidate A supplies an accounting-compatible forcing interface but no incident-power law. Candidate B is a closed finite oscillator and is not a model of an external radiative sun.

The derivation must define the radiation-to-field coupling mechanism, its state variables/port behavior, energy ownership for both the intercepted and non-intercepted fractions, retarded-time integration, and a transaction that reconciles emitted energy, energy arriving at the plane, field energy, and any energy still in transit. It must preserve the 50% integrated interception result without double counting.

### 3.2 Localized in-plane hotspot

**Approved geometry and clock**
- The hotspot is a localized distributed source embedded in the plane.
- Its radial-cosine profile is the initial spatial candidate.
- The profile is normalized over its defined physical support area; total source power must not depend on grid resolution, grid alignment, or recipient count.
- The carrier completes 880 cycles per 24,000-tick day.
- The source requires a finite owned budget or an explicit replenishment origin and schedule.

The normalized profile \(g_h\) satisfies

\[
\int_{\Omega_h}g_h(\mathbf{x})\,dA=1,
\qquad
q_h(\mathbf{x},t)=P_h(t)g_h(\mathbf{x}),
\]

where \(q_h\) is a candidate incident/source power density with units \(E/(L^2\,tick)\). PR #426 validates profile normalization and physical-area integration. It does not establish the physical power-to-field coupling.

**Unresolved coupling question:** The hotspot must be classified as an actively driven finite-budget source, a reciprocal powered oscillator/distributed oscillator system, or another explicitly defined physical source. A prescribed Candidate A waveform is not automatically a prescribed positive power source. Candidate B demonstrates one conservative oscillator/field coupling, but its \(M,\omega,\kappa\) parameters have not been mapped to hotspot power, finite budget, replenishment, or radial-cosine support.

The derivation must decide whether returned field work is accepted by the hotspot, how its source inventory evolves, how the normalized profile enters the coupling law, and how total emitted/returned work reconciles independent of grid and matter count. Do not copy the legacy hotspot rate or convert it through wall-clock seconds.

### 3.3 Powered material oscillator

**Approved ontology**
- A material-origin emitter is a powered oscillator, not a passive resonance score.
- It may emit only from energy owned internally by that material.
- Passive absorption, reflection, transmission, and scattering are separate material-response mechanisms.
- Resonance is response, not an energy source.

Candidate B is the closest current mathematical analogue: a finite oscillator exchanges energy reciprocally with the field, and the total invariant explicitly includes field energy, oscillator energy, and coupling-potential energy.

**Remaining material-specific work:** Define how the oscillator's mechanical/internal state is represented by the material, how its energy is included exactly once in the material-internal inventory, and how the coupling-potential term is assigned without double counting. Derive the mapping from material properties and owned internal energy to oscillator parameters. Extend the isolated model to multiple oscillators and verify stable deterministic energy exchange and atomic material-state/field commits. Passive material response must not be made emissive by this work.

Candidate B's current one-oscillator tests do not validate multiple material emitters, arbitrary material geometry, passive receiver coupling, or production ledger integration.

## 5. Required Phase 3B.3 decisions and tests

Work in this order; do not start production implementation.

1. **Write a source contract for each class.** State physical source type, source state, owner, units, schedule, geometry measure, finite inventory/replenishment, whether returned work is possible, and the destination of all emitted/unused energy.
2. **Derive the source-to-field mechanism.** Show how the source state produces \(f\) or an equivalent incoming field/port state, and derive the resulting signed work. Do not identify incident power density with forcing.
3. **Close the time accounting.** For the sun, include retarded-time mapping and in-transit radiation; for hotspot/material oscillators, use deterministic tick/substep ownership.
4. **Close the spatial accounting.** Integrate the sun over physical plane area; the hotspot over physical support area; material oscillators over their actual coupling footprint. Demonstrate invariance under grid refinement and translation.
5. **Close the global inventory.** Reconcile source energy, field energy, coupling energy, in-transit energy, material internal energy, thermal energy, and the external non-intercepted sun fraction without overlap or unexplained residual.
6. **Derive numerical requirements.** State stability bounds, resolution needs, convergence metrics, and tolerances before running tests.
7. **Run isolated analytical/reference tests.** Include zero-coupling limits, known flux/power cases, sign reversal/returned work where physically allowed, source budget exhaustion, invalid transaction rollback, and repeated multi-source conservation.
8. **Only after review, propose an implementation slice.** No live source registration, receiver/material settlement, chemistry, organism, GUI, or production ledger migration is included in this phase.

## 7. Exit criteria

Phase 3B.3 is complete only when:
- Each of the three source classes has a dimensionally consistent physical coupling law.
- Every source's authoritative energy owner, finite budget or replenishment, and returned-work behavior are explicit.
- Source-to-field work reconciles with emitted/incident energy, including retarded travel and in-transit inventory for the sun.
- The sun's integrated planar interception and external remainder reconcile to total emission.
- Hotspot integrated power is independent of grid refinement, grid alignment, and receiver count.
- Material oscillator energy and coupling potential are represented once in the unified inventory.
- Independent analytic/reference tests validate conservation, deterministic behavior, rollback, and convergence.
- The design is reviewed before any implementation is authorized.
